# ORBIT research specification

Status: **candidate method, not a state-of-the-art claim**.  ORBIT is the working name for
*Orthonormal Reduced-Basis Iterative Target conditionals*.  This document fixes the mathematical
claim boundary before confirmatory experiments.

## Research question

Under TERA's stated stationary-kernel and compatible-noise assumptions, it exactly reduces a scalar
target conditional with `m` neighbouring value/gradient observations to `m²` target-centred
directional derivatives.  Its released implementation materializes and
factorizes the resulting `m² × m²` covariance, giving `O(m⁶)` factorization time and `O(m⁴)` storage
per target.  This makes a larger neighbourhood statistically attractive but computationally
inaccessible.

ORBIT asks whether the same local Gaussian conditional can be evaluated without materializing that
matrix, so that the saved resource can be spent on a larger `m`.  A same-`m` tie with TERA is a
correctness control.  A useful result requires better predictive quality at a matched, analytically
counted resource envelope.

## 1. Exact orthonormal form

For one target `x*`, collect the scaled target-to-neighbour differences in

\[
    \Delta=[\tilde x_1-\tilde x_*,\ldots,\tilde x_m-\tilde x_*]
    \in\mathbb R^{d\times m},
\]

and write its thin singular-value decomposition as

\[
    \Delta=U_r S_r V_r^\top, \qquad r=\operatorname{rank}(\Delta).
\]

TERA observes, at every neighbour `a`, the projected gradient

\[
    q_a=\Delta^\top h_a=V_rS_r(U_r^\top h_a),
\]

where `h_a` is the gradient expressed in scaled-input coordinates.  Define the orthonormal statistic
`z_a=U_rᵀh_a`.  The maps

\[
    z_a=S_r^{-1}V_r^\top q_a, \qquad q_a=V_rS_r z_a
\]

are inverse maps on the support of `q_a`.  Consequently the sigma-algebras generated by all `q_a`
and all `z_a` coincide: replacing **TERA's projected statistics** by `z_a` changes neither its scalar
conditional mean nor variance.  This coordinate equivalence holds even where TERA's projection is
only an approximation to conditioning on the full noisy gradient.  No ambient `d × r` basis must be
retained in the operator state.  The current numerically stable implementation computes a thin SVD
of `Delta` directly, transiently obtaining its left singular vectors, but stores only `V_r`, `S_r`,
and the resulting `m × r` coordinates.  If

\[
    H=\Delta^\top\Delta=V_rS_r^2V_r^\top,
\]

then the required local coordinates are simply `C=V_rS_r`, and observed rows transform as
`Z=Q V_r S_r⁻¹`.

Direct SVD rank detection is intentional: inferring rank from `H` first would square the condition
number and can discard singular directions that remain numerically resolvable.  The kernel distances
and radial coefficients always use the full `DeltaᵀDelta`; an explicitly truncated basis changes
only the retained statistics, not the underlying kernel geometry.

This is an exact removal of algebraic redundancy only.  For generic high-dimensional neighbourhoods
with `d ≥ m`, `r=m`; in that regime there is no dimensional compression.  Dropping any positive mode
is a distinct approximate method and is not part of `ORBIT-exact`.

Noise is part of the claim boundary.  Isotropic raw-gradient noise remains compatible with an
isotropic lengthscale.  With ARD, exact sufficiency for the full noisy gradient requires a
metric-matched raw noise covariance proportional to `diag(lengthscale^-2)`, which becomes isotropic
in scaled-gradient coordinates.  The released TERA `scaled` option instead implements a different
`diag(lengthscale^2)` convention.  ORBIT exposes `iid`, vendor-compatible `scaled`, and
`metric_matched` modes separately; the first two guarantee same-statistic TERA equivalence, not
unqualified full-gradient-GP equivalence under arbitrary ARD.

## 2. Structured Schur-complement operator

For a stationary radial kernel, write the covariance of scaled gradients at neighbours `a,b` as

\[
    A_{ab}I+B_{ab}(C_a-C_b)(C_a-C_b)^\top.
\]

After conditioning on the neighbouring function values, the reduced-gradient covariance is

\[
    K_\delta=G-QK_{ff}^{-1}Q^\top,
\]

with blocks

\[
\begin{aligned}
    G_{ab} &= A_{ab}I_r+B_{ab}(C_a-C_b)(C_a-C_b)^\top
             +\mathbf 1_{a=b}N,\\
    Q_{(a,:),b} &= -A_{ab}(C_a-C_b).
\end{aligned}
\]

Reshape a vector into `X ∈ R^(m × r)`.  Each of `G X`, `Qᵀ vec(X)`, and `Q s` can be evaluated with
matrix products involving only `m × m`, `m × r`, and `r × r` arrays.  With a prefactored `Kff`, one
exact `Kδ` matrix-vector product therefore costs

\[
    O(m^2r+mr^2+m^2)
\]

and uses `O(m²+mr+r²)` operator state.  ORBIT solves `Kδ w=kδ` by PCG.  With `J` iterations, the local
solve costs

\[
    O\!\left(J(m^2r+mr^2)+dm^2+m^3+r^3\right),
\]

where the non-iterative terms include local geometry, the function-value factor, and basis or
preconditioner setup.  The displayed `dm²` SVD term assumes the target regime `d ≥ m`; in general
thin-SVD setup is `O(dm min(d,m))`.  At full rank this is `O(Jm³)` rather than a dense `O(m⁶)` factorization.  The
exact-arithmetic worst case `J≤m²` is `O(m⁵)`; ORBIT does **not** claim unconditional `O(m³)`
inference.

The current fixed preconditioner keeps the dominant Kronecker sum `A ⊗ I_r + I_m ⊗ N`, diagonalized
by the separate eigensystems of `A` and `N`.  The radial outer-product term and function-value Schur
correction remain in the PCG operator.  Its usefulness is an empirical hypothesis and must be
ablated.

## 3. Anytime conservative posterior and certificate

First condition on the neighbouring values.  Let scalar target `Y` and reduced gradient vector `Z`
be jointly Gaussian with

\[
    \operatorname{Var}(Y)=v_0,\quad
    \operatorname{Cov}(Z)=\Sigma\succ0,\quad
    \operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)=k.
\]

For any search subspace with basis `S`, define

\[
    w_*=\Sigma^{-1}k, \qquad
    w_S=S(S^\top\Sigma S)^{-1}S^\top k.
\]

The Galerkin approximation is not merely a numerical plug-in: it is exactly the Gaussian
conditional given the compressed observation `SᵀZ`,

\[
    Y\mid S^\top Z\sim
    \mathcal N\!\left(\mu_0+w_S^\top(Z-\mu_Z),\;v_S\right),
    \qquad v_S=v_0-k^\top w_S.
\]

Writing `δS=||w*-wS||²_Σ` gives

\[
    v_S=v_*+\delta_S, \qquad
    \mathbb E[(\mu_* - \mu_S)^2]=\delta_S,
\]

and hence

\[
    \mathbb E_Z\!\left[
      \operatorname{KL}\{p(Y\mid Z)\,\|\,p(Y\mid S^\top Z)\}
    \right]
    =\tfrac12\log(v_S/v_*).
\]

Zero-start PCG with a fixed symmetric positive-definite preconditioner produces just such Galerkin
subspaces.  Every iterate therefore defines a valid conservative scalar conditional; early stopping
inflates rather than silently understates its mathematical variance in exact arithmetic.

Finite-precision PCG does not preserve Galerkin orthogonality exactly.  Therefore the implementation
does not use the cancellation-only expression `v0-kᵀw` for an approximate iterate.  It evaluates

\[
    v(w)=v_0-2k^\top w+w^\top\Sigma w
        =v_0-k^\top w-w^\top e,
\]

using the recomputed residual.  For any weights `w`, not just an ideal Galerkin iterate,

\[
    v(w)-v_*=\lVert w-w_*\rVert_\Sigma^2\ge0.
\]

Thus the energy expression is conservative for any stored iterate when evaluated in exact
arithmetic, rather than only for an ideal Galerkin iterate.  IEEE evaluation is still subject to
ordinary rounding: an fp32 audit found one-ulp-scale violations (about `3e-8`) of literal inequalities
against a float64 diagnostic solve.  The implementation and experiments therefore describe this as
conservative **up to floating-point roundoff**, and do not present sub-resolution bounds as rigorous
interval certificates.  When Galerkin orthogonality holds, the expression reduces to the exact
compressed-observation conditional above.

For the true residual `e=k-ΣwS`,

\[
    \delta_S=e^\top\Sigma^{-1}e.
\]

If `Σ ⪰ λ0 I`, then `B=||e||²/λ0` bounds `δS`.  When `B<vS`,

\[
    \mathbb E[\operatorname{KL}]
    \le \tfrac12\log\!\left(\frac{v_S}{v_S-B}\right).
\]

The implementation recomputes the structured residual after PCG rather than certifying from the
recursive residual, and uses the same residual in the variance correction.  TERA's `εI`
regularizer is transformed from `q` coordinates to
`ε S_r^-2 = ε(V_rS_r^-1)^T(V_rS_r^-1)` in orthonormal coordinates; adding `εI` directly after the
basis change would define a different numerical posterior.  The generally available `λ0` is the
smallest eigenvalue of transformed projected observation noise plus this transformed regularizer.
With no strict lower bound, ORBIT reports the certificate as unavailable.  The certificate controls
the PCG solve only.  If positive geometric modes were truncated, it is explicitly marked as not a
certificate to the exact-rank posterior.  All certificate inequalities are exact-arithmetic results
for the represented operator; the reported floating-point numbers carry the roundoff qualification
above.  The bound is observation-averaged; it is not a pointwise KL guarantee for an adversarial
realised gradient vector.

A future IEEE-rigorous implementation need not materialize `Sigma`, but it must obtain verified
enclosures.  If `||e-e_hat|| <= eta_r`, `|v_hat-v(w)| <= eta_v`, and the lower spectral bound
`lambda0` is itself certified, then a safe reported variance and gap bound are

\[
    v_{safe}=v_{hat}+\eta_v,\qquad
    B=\frac{(\lVert\hat e\rVert+\eta_r)^2}{\lambda_0}+2\eta_v.
\]

When `B < v_safe`, substituting these quantities gives the valid upper bound
`0.5 log(v_safe/(v_safe-B))`.  Missing enclosure errors must mean “not floating-point rigorous,”
never silently default to zero.  ORBIT's current API therefore reports
`exact_arithmetic_certified` separately and always sets `floating_point_rigorous=False`.

## 4. What is and is not new

The strongest defensible framing is **matrix-free, computation-aware TERA**.  We are not aware of
prior work combining TERA's target-specific sufficient gradient statistics, an exact structured
matrix-free application of their function-conditioned covariance, and a per-factor
residual-to-expected-KL stopping diagnostic.  That search result is not proof of priority, so the
claim is stated as this TERA-specific synthesis rather than as an unconditional "first":

- TERA-specific exact orthonormal coordinates and a structured matrix-free realization of its local
  Schur complement;
- an anytime interpretation of its PCG iterates as conservative scalar Vecchia factors;
- a TERA-specific residual-to-expected-posterior-KL stopping certificate;
- empirical use of the saved resource for larger conditioning sets.

The following are prior art and must not be claimed as new:

- iterative derivative-GP inference and structured derivative-kernel matrix-vector products
  ([Eriksson et al., 2018](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/c2f32522a84d5e6357e6abac087f1b0b-Abstract.html),
  [de Roos et al., 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/de-roos21a.html),
  [Ament and Gomes, 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/ament22a.html));
- computational uncertainty for iterative GP inference
  ([Bartels and Hennig, 2020](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/18-473.html),
  [Reid et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03225),
  [Wenger et al., 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/4683beb6bab325650db13afd05d1a14a-Abstract-Conference.html));
- general goal-oriented or information-theoretic Gaussian observation reduction
  ([Spantini et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.01881),
  [Giraldi et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06606));
- a soft-interpolation derivative GP.  DSoftKI learns a finite interpolation basis and its Jacobian,
  whereas ORBIT preserves TERA's stationary-kernel local conditional and changes only its numerical
  representation and solve ([Huang, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09134)).

The novelty statement remains provisional until a broader citation and contemporary-code search is
complete.  “First derivative-GP PCG”, “first KL-controlled Gaussian projection”, and “`O(m³)` exact
inference” are explicitly out of scope.

## 5. Falsification and evaluation ladder

1. **Algebra:** random fp64 dense fixtures must match the matrix-free operator, and exact-rank ORBIT
   must match released TERA means and variances at the same neighbours to `1e-6`.
2. **Solver:** every recorded solve must meet its recomputed residual tolerance; non-finite or
   non-positive raw variances are failures, never clipped successes.  The residual certificate is
   checked against dense energy error on small fixtures.
3. **Mechanism:** at least three paired exact-GP simulations must show that a nontrivial larger
   neighbourhood (`m<n`) reduces average marginal KL.  An `m=n` exact-recovery endpoint is only a
   sanity check.
4. **Resource matching:** choose ORBIT's `m`, tolerance, and preconditioner on validation data under
   fixed analytic counts for kernel work, linear algebra, and live tensor state.  Isolated-node wall
   time and accelerator memory are descriptive corroboration, not the matching rule.
5. **Predictive claim:** on leakage-free grouped N-body replicas, require both at least 5% paired
   standardized-value RMSE reduction and at least 0.05 nat/point NLL improvement, with hierarchical
   95% intervals excluding zero and no material dimension-wise regression.
6. **Generality:** repeat the direction of effect on TERA's official GP-simulation and MD22 splits;
   compare official DSoftKI and DDSVGP in addition to TERA.  Until this passes, call ORBIT a research
   prototype rather than a superior GP framework.

Hyperparameter learning is not yet implemented.  Krylov spaces and stopping decisions must either be
frozen inside each optimization step or differentiated with their parameter dependence handled
explicitly; otherwise TERA's score-estimation argument cannot simply be inherited.
